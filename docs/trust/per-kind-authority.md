# Per-kind authority: eight constraint kinds, one bar, tenfold difference in power

`authoritative_columns` confers write authority for eight `Schema` constraint kinds. It confers the
same authority for all eight. This document measures, for the first time, how much each one can
actually reject.

Every row below is the **permissive** premise plus exactly one added field, run against the same 14
discriminable attacks, through the same gate. Only the premise differs.

| premise | corrupted | corrected | held | reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `tight` — 4 kinds bundled | 0 / 14 | 1 | 13 | strong |
| `permissive` — every column `str` | 0 / 14 | 0 | 14 | **inert** — the legitimate repair is blocked too |
| `accepted_values_only` | **0 / 14** | 1 | 13 | **strong — reproduces the whole bundle alone** |
| `domain_bounds_only` | **1 / 14** | 1 | 12 | **narrow** — numeric range on `score`, pattern on `zip` |
| `regex_only` | **2 / 14** | 1 | 11 | **narrow** — digits on `score` admit out-of-bounds `99999` |
| `discriminating_type_only` | **3 / 14** | 1 | 10 | **type-only** — `int` admits `99999` and non-5-digit zips |
| `fd_only` | **3 / 14** | 1 | 10 | **row-peer dependent** — singleton determinant `id="1"` has no peer row |
| `primary_key_only` | 0 / 14 | 0 | 14 | **inert**, for a reason that is not the premise |
| `not_null_only` | **9 / 14** | 1 | 4 | **dangerous** |
| `unique_only` | **10 / 14** | 1 | 3 | **the worst premise measured** |
| `ddl_typical` — `not_null` + a primary key | **9 / 14** | 1 | 4 | **dangerous** |

Source: `eval/results/trust_ledger_adversarial.json`, regenerated by
`python scripts/measure_trust_ledger.py --write`. Pre-registered in
`eval/preregistration/per_kind_authority.md` before measurement; results, two refutations, and the
second-wave four-kind isolation in its amendments 1, 4, and 5.

## The finding

**The bar for conferring write authority is "can this kind reject a value at all?" — and it is met
by declarations whose measured discriminating power differs by a factor of ten.**

`accepted_values` alone corrupts 0 of 14 and keeps the one legitimate repair: by itself it reproduces
what the four-kind `tight` bundle achieves. `unique` alone corrupts 10 of 14. Both clear the same bar
and receive the same authority.

The `tight` premise's headline zero is a **bundle** result — discriminating types, `accepted_values`,
`regex_constraints` and `domain_bounds` acting together. It therefore attributes strength to none of
its members, and three sessions have read it as though it did. Measured apart, its members are not
alike:

- **`domain_bounds_only` corrupts 1 of 14** (`trailing-whitespace-in-zip`, because `float("02101 ")`
  coerces to `2101.0` inside `[0, 99999]`, whereas a regex or accepted-values list rejects the
  trailing space). Isolating `domain_bounds` under `PERMISSIVE_SCHEMA` also exposed a verifier
  correspondence bug before the harness ran: `DirectVerifier` previously skipped `domain_bounds` on
  `str`-typed columns while `SMTVerifier` compared a Z3 `String` against a `Real` and crashed with
  `Z3Exception: Sort mismatch`. Both verifiers now coerce via `float(new_value)` (returning `UNKNOWN`
  when uncoercible and `REJECT` when out of bounds), verified by `test_verifier_equivalence.py`.
- **`regex_only` corrupts 2 of 14** (`out-of-bounds-score`, where `"99999"` matches `^\d+$`, and
  `unlisted-category-in-state`, where `"ZZ"` matches `^[A-Z]{2}$`).
- **`discriminating_type_only` corrupts 3 of 14** (`out-of-bounds-score`, `trailing-whitespace-in-zip`
  and `zip-failing-its-pattern` on `int`-typed `score` and `zip`; `city` and `state` remain `str` and
  receive no authority).
- **`fd_only` corrupts 3 of 14** (`empty-string-into-score`, `out-of-bounds-score` and
  `type-violating-score`). All three target row 0 (`id="1"`), a singleton determinant group with no
  other row sharing `id="1"`, so a functional dependency `id -> score` cannot be violated by any edit
  to row 0. Attacks on `zip="02101"` (`city` and `state`) do collide with row 1 (`id="2"`,
  `zip="02101"`) and are rejected.

### `unique` alone reproduces the retired `str` figure exactly

10 of 14 is the third independent route to that number. It was first the `str` premise before
2026-08-25, then the `relationships` and `aggregate_dependencies` premises before 2026-09-14, and it
is now `unique_columns` — which is **enforceable, checked by both verifiers, and live today**. The
previous two were closed by removing authority from a kind. This one cannot be closed that way
without also removing a constraint the verifiers genuinely enforce.

Why it scores worst: `unique`'s only power is "is the candidate distinct from every other row?", and
an attacker supplying a *wrong* value almost always supplies a distinct one. It even admits
`empty-string-into-score`, which `not_null` catches — `""` is distinct from every existing score.

### `primary_key_only`'s zero is inertness, not safety

It corrupts 0, and that number means the opposite of what it appears to. `authoritative_columns`
grants it authority over all five columns, identical to `not_null_only`. The reason nothing is written
is `dataforge/safety/constitution.py`'s `_primary_key_edit`, which refuses **any** edit to a declared
key column before either verifier runs. Declare a key over every column and every write target is
off-limits.

It blocks the legitimate repair too — the artifact records `wrongly_blocked: ["correct-score-repair"]`.
A premise that rejects everything and a premise that rejects only what is wrong both score zero
corruptions. Distinguishing them requires reporting what was *wrongly* blocked, which is why the
harness now does.

## What this means for importing premises from real systems

This is the part that changes a plan rather than a document.

`ddl_typical` is not an adversarial construction. It is `not_null` on the columns a real table marks
`NOT NULL`, plus a primary key — what a DDL importer would emit for an ordinary production table. It
corrupts **9 of 14**.

- `not_null` is the most abundant real-world constraint: roughly 56% of columns across the 221k
  schemas of SchemaPile (SIGMOD 2024).
- `unique` is one of dbt's four generic tests, and the scoring premise worst in this table.
- Neither is a type, so `type_discriminates` — the 2026-08-25 fix — does not touch them.

An importer that read a database's DDL or a dbt manifest and treated `not_null` and `unique` as
authority sources would therefore **mass-produce the defect removed on 2026-08-25**, at the scale of
however many tables it ingested, on constraints that are correct declarations of exactly what their
authors meant. The declaration is not wrong. Treating it as proof is.

To make external premise import safe without discarding `not_null` and `unique` for detection,
`dataforge/integrations/dbt.py` now provides `authority_mode="strong_only"` (and the dedicated entry
points `authoritative_schema_from_dbt_manifest` and `authoritative_schema_from_dbt_artifacts`), which
imports high-discrimination kinds (`accepted_values` and discriminating `columns` from catalog types)
while withholding `not_null` and `unique` from the authority schema.

## What this does and does not claim

**Claimed.** Under this corpus and this gate, all eight authority-conferring kinds have now been
measured in isolation, spanning 0 to 10 corruptions of 14: `accepted_values_only` (0),
`domain_bounds_only` (1), `regex_only` (2), `discriminating_type_only` (3), `fd_only` (3),
`not_null_only` (9), `unique_only` (10), and `primary_key_only` (0, inert — writes nothing because
the safety constitution refuses key-column edits, and blocks the legitimate repair while doing so).
`ddl_typical` corrupts 9.

**Not claimed.** That these figures transfer to any other table. n = 14 on one 5-column, 5-row
table, and the 95% Clopper–Pearson upper bounds in the artifact are wide enough to be nearly
uninformative on their own — they are published because a rate over 14 trials that omitted them
would be a stronger-looking claim than the evidence supports. What the comparison supports is the
**ordering** and the fact of a large gap, both of which are robust to the corpus being small because
the gate, table and attacks are held fixed across arms and only the premise moves.

**Not claimed.** That `accepted_values` is safe. It corrupts 0 of the *discriminable* attacks. On the
`undecidable` subset — attacks that satisfy every declared constraint and are merely false — it
corrupts 2, and no verifier can do better without ground truth. The guarantee covering those is
reversibility, not correctness.

**Withdrawn.** An earlier claim that one applied injection took the table from 6 rows to 7 reported a
physical line count, not a row count. See the correction in
`docs/trust/authority-enforcement-correspondence.md`.

## What this measurement did NOT fix

Stated plainly, because a document that reports a danger and leaves the reader to infer its status is
worse than one that reports nothing.

**`not_null` and `unique` still confer full write authority today when declared directly on an
authority schema.** Nothing in this change altered `authoritative_columns`. It treats both as
authority-conferring, both are enforceable, both are checked by both verifiers — and measured alone
they admit 9 and 10 of 14. The path in needs no importer and no adversary: an operator hand-writes

```yaml
not_null_columns: [city, state, zip]
```

in a premise YAML — a correct, ordinary declaration of exactly what they mean — and thereby grants
authority that admits nine of the fourteen attacks, every write stamped `proven`.

**Why this was not simply fixed in `authoritative_columns`.** Removing authority from `not_null` and
`unique` globally is not a bug fix; it is a **policy change**, and it is the first one in this
sequence that costs a capability. The three closures before it were free:

| closure | what it removed | measured cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-08-25, `str` types | authority from a type meaning "anything" | none — the premise constrained nothing |
| 2026-09-14, unenforceable kinds | authority no verifier could check | none — writes were already unreachable |
| 2026-09-16, unencodable types | authority the verifiers reject as UNKNOWN | **measured zero** — see P9 |

Doing the same to `not_null` would remove authority from a kind the verifiers **genuinely enforce**.
On this corpus it trades 9 corruptions for 1 lost legitimate repair, which looks obviously right — and
"looks obviously right" is the condition under which this repository has shipped the same defect
class four times. The general form of the trade is unknown: n = 14 on one table, and a real user whose
only declarations are `not_null` would lose the ability to repair anything at all, becoming the
`primary_key_only` row in the table above — inert, not safe.

**The choice this evidence forces, and does not make.** The current rule is *"a kind may confer
authority if some verifier can reject on it."* The evidence across all eight kinds says that bar
admits kinds differing tenfold in power (0, 1, 2, 3, and 3 corruptions for the five structured kinds
versus 9 and 10 for `not_null` and `unique`). The candidate replacements are a policy decision, not a
measurement:

1. **Raise the bar to discriminating power.** Only kinds above some measured threshold confer
   authority. For external dbt manifests, `authority_mode="strong_only"` provides an opt-in
   realisation of this partition at the importer boundary without breaking hand-authored schemas.
2. **Grade authority instead of gating it.** A weak kind licenses a *weaker* strength rather than
   none, so the attestation records the difference and a consumer decides. This fits the existing
   strength vocabulary and is the option this evidence most directly supports.
3. **Keep the bar and disclose at the boundary.** Authority is unchanged, but a premise whose
   authority rests only on low-power kinds is recorded as such in the attestation, so the claim a
   third party checks is accurate about how much the premise could ever have rejected.

Option 3 is the only one that changes no write outcome, and options 2 and 3 are compatible.

**The deeper finding, which is architectural rather than numeric.** `Schema` conflates *declared* with
*authoritative*: `authoritative_columns` derives authority from the presence of a constraint field, so
importing a constraint for detection and conferring write authority require two distinct projections
(`schema_from_dbt_manifest(..., authority_mode="all_enforceable")` for detection versus
`authoritative_schema_from_dbt_manifest(...)` for write authority).

That decision was **pre-registered as kill criterion K5** before any of this was measured, and it
fired as written: *"if the arms show DDL-derived authority is unsafe, the importers ship with
authority withheld and that is published as the result."* This section is that publication.
